When Steam Triggered a Priority 1 Suricata Alert

Status: BenignOctober 2026

What I Saw

I was testing my Suricata IDS when WatcherDash suddenly lit up red with a Priority 1 alert. I wasn't deliberately testing Steam at the time, so seeing an unexpected high-priority alert was a bit of a scare.

Investigation

The alert was ET USER_AGENTS Steam HTTP Client User-Agent (SID 2028651). I checked the alert details and the surrounding HTTP activity in Suricata's logs. The traffic came from my Windows workstation, and Steam had started automatically without me noticing.

The signature was matching Steam's HTTP User-Agent, not demonstrating that a compromise had occurred. The alert's priority was worth investigating, but it didn't replace the need for context.

What It Turned Out to Be

Normal Steam traffic. Once I identified the source, the surprising alert became useful: I could open Steam again to test WatcherDash's pinned red-alert display with recognizable traffic.

Why It Mattered

My first instinct was to investigate the Priority 1 alert, not assume it represented a real attack. A high-priority rule match can still have an ordinary explanation. Also, I accidentally found a convenient repeatable test for my dashboard. Not bad for a surprise from a game client.
