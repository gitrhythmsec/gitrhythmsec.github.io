The Outbound SSH Scan That Wasn't

Status: ResolvedOctober 29–30, 2026

What I Saw

Suricata reported ET SCAN Potential SSH Scan OUTBOUND (SID 2003068, Priority 2) from a Raspberry Pi hosting my UniFi controller. The destination was an unfamiliar private address outside my normal LAN. I had no reason to expect that Pi to be scanning anything.

Investigation

I checked the Suricata flow details. The packets targeted TCP 22, with no responses visible in the captured flow. A route lookup showed the Pi would send traffic for that address through the normal gateway; it didn't indicate a mysterious second local network.

Then I traced the UniFi OS Server process into its rootless Podman container. Searching the application logs inside the container revealed repeated messages like:

SSH adopt failed ip[X.X.X.X], msg[unreachable]

The logged adoption attempts lined up closely with Suricata's SSH timestamps, and the application logs identified my managed switch model. I also verified that the switch was currently adopted, online, and working normally.

What It Turned Out to Be

UniFi controller adoption attempts, not an unknown host scanning the network. The exact reason the controller retried that old/default address wasn't established, but the origin of the traffic was clear.

Why It Mattered

A scan signature can describe legitimate management behavior. Correlating a network alert with application logs gave me a much stronger explanation than the alert name alone.
