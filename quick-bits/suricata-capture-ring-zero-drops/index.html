Fixing Suricata Packet Drops with AF_PACKET Ring Tuning

Status: ResolvedNovember 2026

What I Saw

My Suricata IDS was running on a dedicated Debian sensor connected to a mirrored switch port. I was generating and investigating alerts, but its capture statistics showed a serious problem: the sensor was dropping packets. That's not something I want from an IDS, especially if I'm using its alerts to decide whether something happened on my network.

Investigation

I worked through the capture problem and adjusted Suricata's AF_PACKET ring configuration. Rather than judging the fix by whether WatcherDash looked busy, I checked the actual capture counters in Suricata's statistics log.

I used commands like:

sudo tail -n 200 /var/log/suricata/stats.log | \
  grep -E 'capture.kernel_packets|capture.kernel_drops' | tail

Before tuning, the sensor had reported substantial kernel packet loss. At some points, nearly one in three packets was being dropped. After the change, I watched the counters while the IDS continued monitoring normal network traffic. At one checkpoint, Suricata had processed more than 12 million packets over roughly three days, and the reported kernel-drop counter remained at zero. At the time of writing, the counter had reached 16.2 million packets, still with zero reported kernel drops.

What It Turned Out to Be

The capture-ring tuning resolved the packet-drop problem in the monitoring period I measured. Suricata continued processing millions of packets without reporting additional kernel drops.

I can't say exactly which earlier events were missed because of packet loss. In particular, I later observed a router-login alert that I hadn't seen since starting Suricata, but I don't have evidence directly proving that the ring size was the reason.

If you want to see the current (within the last two hours) packet count, along with drops, visit the homelab page! ^

Why It Mattered

A dashboard can be full of alerts while the sensor behind it is still missing traffic. This was a good lesson to validate the capture pipeline itself, not just the detection rules. The statistic I cared about wasn't how many alerts appeared; it was whether Suricata was keeping up with the packets delivered to it.
