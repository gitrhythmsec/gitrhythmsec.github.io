Why My IDS Missed a Full Nmap Scan

Status: InformationalNovember 6, 2026

What I Saw

I ran a full TCP port scan against a Raspberry Pi in my lab. Nmap checked all 65,535 TCP ports and found three open, but Suricata and WatcherDash didn't alert on the scan. That was a little embarrassing for an IDS I had just been testing.

Investigation

I compared Nmap's successful results with Suricata's view and ran tcpdump on the IDS capture interface while testing again. The capture showed the target Pi communicating with internet hosts, but not the local scan probes I expected.

My IDS was using a mirrored switch port associated with the router uplink. The scanning machine and target were on the same local network, so their packets could be switched directly between local ports without crossing that uplink.

What It Turned Out to Be

A likely monitoring visibility gap, not proof that Suricata's detection rules failed. The captured traffic was consistent with uplink mirroring, although the available test didn't establish the switch's complete forwarding behavior. The sensor cannot alert on packets it never receives.

Why It Mattered

Seeing internet traffic wasn't the same as seeing every LAN conversation. I chose to keep the existing uplink-focused setup for now, while recognizing that local east-west traffic can be missed and that even captured traffic doesn't guarantee an alert.
